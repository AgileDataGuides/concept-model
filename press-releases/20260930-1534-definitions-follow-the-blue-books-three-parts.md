# Concept Definitions Now Follow the Blue Book's Three Parts

**30 September 2026**

AgileDataGuides today released three-part Definitions and a Definitions tab for the Concept Model app, so every Concept says what it is, gives real examples, and settles its special cases, in words a newcomer can read.

## The Problem

"What do we mean by Customer?" is the question that saves the most trouble later, and the app gave it one text box. Teams wrote a single line, or a formal category-and-differentiator sentence that only the people in the room could follow. There was no place for the examples that make a Definition concrete, or for the special cases where the arguments hide: is a prospect on a free trial a Customer? Nor was there a way to mark which Definitions the expert had agreed and which were still in dispute, so a half-finished Definition looked the same as a settled one.

## The Solution

Step 5 - Agree the Definitions now captures the book's three parts for each Concept: the nature of the thing in everyday words, a few real examples, and the special cases and points of confusion. Each Definition is marked Draft, Agreed or Flagged, so the team can agree the easy ones, flag the hard ones and keep moving. The category-and-differentiator sentence stays as an optional helper. The new Definitions tab reads the whole Model one thing at a time, Concepts grouped by Domain with their three parts and status, then each Relationship and each Core Business Event.

## How It Works

Pick a Concept from the list, write its three parts, and set its status. The step's status line counts the Agreed and Flagged Definitions. A quiet hint appears when a Definition leans on words like table, flag or status code, because the book says a Definition that needs them describes software, not the organisation. Part one is still the Concept's description, so the Business Glossary and every other canvas keep reading it.

## Key Benefits

- **Definitions anyone can read**: plain words, real examples, and the special cases written down before they cause an argument
- **Progress you can see**: Agreed and Flagged at a glance, so a session knows what is settled
- **A deliverable of its own**: the Definitions tab is the book's second pattern template, ready to share
- **Nothing lost**: existing descriptions and category-and-differentiator Definitions carry straight over

Three-part Definitions are available now in the Concept Model app at [github.com/AgileDataGuides/concept-model](https://github.com/AgileDataGuides/concept-model) and in the [live demo](https://agiledataguides.github.io/concept-model/).
